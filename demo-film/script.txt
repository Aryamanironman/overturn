This is OVERTURN. It answers a health-insurance denial with evidence. This is the system running.
Ava is thirty-four. Northbridge Health denied her lumbar MRI. Four reasons. Three are contradicted by the file.
Every fact comes through one door: Zetaris. Seven tables: policy, denial, medical record, benefits, procedure codes, claim documents and diagnosis codes.
A planner queries. A reconciler builds the ledger. A critic attacks it. An advocate argues the insurer's side. Either can force a re-query. A deterministic scorer grades it, and a reporter writes the packet.
One command runs the loop. This run is sample mode: same control flow, deterministic stubs, SQLite mirror. Every step is traced.
Reason one: they call the MRI experimental. The clause they cite excludes spinal fusion, not diagnostic imaging. The clause that applies covers this scan. Rebutted.
Reason two: they cite the contrast study, which needs prior authorisation. The order and the record use the non-contrast code. Code mismatch. Rebutted.
Reason three: no conservative treatment documented. The record shows twelve physiotherapy sessions over twelve weeks. The policy asks for six. Rebutted.
Reason four: a specialist letter is missing. It is genuinely missing. OVERTURN names the exact document and refuses to invent it. The appeal is conditional, not perfect.
Forty-seven evidence lines. Each carries its source row, its status, and a SHA-256 hash chained to the line before it. Change one row and the chain breaks.
Remove a source and the score tells you what it was worth. Pull the policy, the record, or the codes, and eighty-four drops to sixty-five.
Zetaris is the only data path: one tool, run SQL. Meterless H-MEM remembers the case across runs. The World Model is the shared ledger. Nightshift is the primary model provider, with critic and advocate pinned to NVIDIA Nemotron.
The output is one document a human can send: cover letter, four cited rebuttals, evidence map, and one action left — get the letter.
Eighty-four. Solid. Conditional. Supply the letter, and it becomes one hundred. The denial, answered.
